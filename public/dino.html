dino · the first pixling

Dino, up close.

A palm-sized printed body, about 7.5 cm tall, with a screen for a face. Here is everything we can say about it today.

01 · at a glance

The short version

Dino sits on your desk and keeps you company. It blinks, peeks, gets sleepy and shows you the time on a double-tap. There is no app to set up and no account to make.

02 · what it does

Small things, done well

Dino is built around a few simple jobs, plus a personality that makes you want to keep it on your desk.

time
Clock, alarm and stopwatchDouble-tap and Dino shows you the time on its face. The clock shifts by a pixel every few minutes to protect the screen.
focus
Focus, togetherPick 25, 45 or 60 minutes. When the time is up, Dino leads you through a short breathing break.
mood
One face, 24 expressionsHappy, excited, sleepy, mischievous. The whole personality lives in the face.
quirks
Charming, never needyEvery few minutes it does a tiny rawr. Leave it alone for five minutes and it dozes off. Any touch wakes it.
coming later soon
Tiny gamesMeteor dodge, snack snap, desk pong and hot egg, arriving in a later update.
coming later soon
Friends by bumpKnock two Dinos together to pair them, then send a rawr.
03 · what's inside

Three printed parts. A tiny computer inside.

A body, a screen bezel and a back plate, printed in matte PLA. Inside is everything Dino needs to notice you: a screen, a touch pad, motion sensing and a rechargeable battery charged over USB-C.

Equally important is what is not inside. No camera. No microphone. Dino is a companion, not a sensor, and it works without an account.
